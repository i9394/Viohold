How to Save M3U8 / HLS Web Videos on iPhone (Without Shortcuts or a PC)

Why Safari cannot save .m3u8 streaming playlists directly, and how to save supported MP4 and HLS web videos with readable filenames for offline playback on iPhone and iPad.

Applies to: iOS 18+ & iPadOS 18+ Updated: November 2026

If you have ever tried to save a web video on an iPhone using Safari, you have probably run into two dead ends: either there is no download button at all, or the site streams video as an HLS (.m3u8) playlist split into dozens of small segments that Safari cannot save directly to Files.

Most workarounds on iOS require opening a web inspector, copying an .m3u8 URL into a fragile iOS Shortcut, or moving the link to a Mac or PC to run yt-dlp and ffmpeg.

Why Standard Browsers Struggle with M3U8 Streams

Unlike a single .mp4 file, an .m3u8 stream is a text manifest listing short media segments (often .ts or .m4s) and sometimes an AES-128 decryption key. Saving the .m3u8 file alone only gives you a tiny text file that will not play offline.

To play an HLS stream offline on iPhone or iPad, an app has to:

  1. Detect the active stream while the webpage loads or plays;
  2. Download the media segments reliably;
  3. Merge and convert into a standard playable .mp4 file when needed;
  4. Give it a readable filename instead of index.m3u8 or master_720p.mp4.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why can't Safari download M3U8 videos directly on iPhone?

An M3U8 URL points to an HLS text playlist made of many small video segments rather than a single MP4 file. Safari plays the stream but does not merge the segments into an offline file.

How do I save an M3U8 or MP4 web video with Cove Browser?

Open the supported webpage in Cove Browser, start playing the video, press and hold directly on the video, and choose Save Video. Cove downloads and converts supported streams into playable offline files with automatically detected titles.

No. Cove Browser does not support downloading from YouTube, Netflix, TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, X, Vimeo, or DRM-protected sites.
